# Label Mutants Syntactically, Treat Depth as Hypothesis

For a benchmark of formally filtered, single-fault Verilog mutants, the label that holds up is a **syntactic taxonomy assigned automatically from the mutation operator**. Use VeriBugBench's five construct families (Expression, Bit-vector & access, Assignment, Timing, Port). They are derived from Meng et al.'s mining of 1,298 real bug-fix commits. Cross-reference them to the MSR'08 fix-pattern names, and add a small rule-derived semantic tag (reset branch, FSM next-state, condition vs. right-hand side) borrowed from Ma et al.'s FPGA bug study. Semantic root-cause schemes such as Ma et al., HWE-Bench, and CWE need human or LLM labelling, and on small single-module designs nearly everything ends up as "functional logic". The link between depth and difficulty is **not established**. The only quantitative hardware evidence is one table in RTL-Repair (ASPLOS 2024): the three bugs whose state-to-output divergence exceeded 25 cycles went unrepaired by both tools, but many shallow bugs also failed. That pattern is necessary-but-not-sufficient, rests on n = 3, and is confounded with design size. No LLM debugging paper reports success by cycles-to-failure, so depth must be framed as a pre-registered hypothesis with design size, operator family, and sequentiality controlled. Existing LLM RTL debug benchmarks stratify difficulty by proxies (syntax vs. functional, module type, code size, pass-rate tiers) and grade with testbenches. None found grades with formal equivalence, none uses minimum counterexample depth as a difficulty axis, and none crosses difficulty with an agent-vs-single-shot comparison. These are three defensible novelty claims, each resting on absence of evidence. The largest threat is contamination. VerilogEval and RTLLM show near-100% contamination signals for GPT-3.5/GPT-4o. Surface disguise recovers only a few points in comparable software repair studies. And EQY grading gives full credit to a model that simply recalls the golden design. So the mitigations with evidence are semantic changes, fresh controls, cheap probes, and stratified reporting.

Throughout, verification flags from the research notes are kept in brackets: **[full text]** means the primary paper was read; **[abstract only]**, **[snippet-only]**, or **[secondary]** means only an abstract, search snippet, or third-party summary was seen. Statements marked as *inference* are synthesis, not published findings.

## Five syntactic families can be assigned by rule; semantic root causes cannot

Every empirical study of real RTL bugs agrees on the same rough shape: **assignment and expression errors are the plurality (roughly 30–55% of fixes), conditionals come next (roughly 18–25%), and sensitivity-list or edge errors are a few percent**. The MSR'08 study of four OpenCores projects (UART16550, OC8051, OR1K, Mem_ctrl) defined 25 fix patterns in 7 categories. Assignment-related patterns were 29–55% of instances, if-related 18–25%, and case statements only 1.8–4.6% [full text] ([Sudhakrishnan et al., MSR 2008](https://masc.soe.ucsc.edu/docs/msr08.pdf)). Meng et al.'s 2025 AST-diff study, which mined 1,298 bug-fix commits from 300 projects, assigns its categories fully automatically. Its expression-level distribution is **infix/prefix/ternary expressions 53.79%, bit-select/part-select 21.39%, assignment 15.13%, edge (rising/falling) 3.28%, and port direction 1.28%** [secondary: read via VeriBugBench's summary because the Springer page was paywalled] ([VeriBugBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18022)). An OpenTitan study of 170 RTL bug fixes found assignments touched in 50.1% of fixes and conditionals in 24.8% [HTML via summarizing fetch] ([arXiv 2402.00684](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00684)).

The semantic schemes tell a richer story but cannot be computed by rule. Ma et al. classified 68 functional bugs across 19 FPGA designs into **Data Mis-Access (41%), Communication (25%), and Semantic (34%)**, with 13 subclasses such as Bit Truncation, Misindexing, Failure-to-Update, and Signal Asynchrony [full text] ([Ma et al., ASPLOS 2022](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10355397)). They labelled by hand and resolved multi-class bugs by choosing "the most related and specific subclass". HWE-Bench assigns its five root-cause classes with an LLM classification agent, and no agreement statistic appeared in the text read ([HWE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14709)). The only inter-rater figure in this area comes from processor errata: RemembERR's two human labellers agreed "generally above 80%" [full text] ([RemembERR](https://comsec.ethz.ch/wp-content/files/rememberr_micro22.pdf)). No study validates a mapping from syntactic pattern to semantic root cause for RTL. The OpenTitan study found "no proportional difference" in AST node types between functional and security fixes, which suggests syntax alone does not predict semantic class ([arXiv 2402.00684](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00684)).

| Taxonomy | Type | Auto-assignable to a mutant? | Fit for ~15 small Verilog-2005 designs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meng 2025 / VeriBugBench 5 families | Syntactic | **Yes, 1:1 with operator** | Best primary label; published real-world prevalence available for comparison |
| MSR'08 (25 patterns, 7 categories) | Syntactic, fix-side | **Yes**, with AST context (enclosing if/case/always; `=` vs `<=`) | Good secondary label; invert direction (fix "add" = mutant "delete"); port/declaration patterns mostly irrelevant |
| Al-Asaad/Van Campenhout leaf classes | Mixed | **Mostly**: wrong operator/constant/signal source, missing inversion; FSM error needs FSM detection; "conceptual error" never | Old student/research CPU data, but frequencies exist |
| Ma et al. 3×13 | Semantic root cause | **Partial**: Bit Truncation, Misindexing, Failure-to-Update, control- vs. data-flow Erroneous Expression by rule; Communication subclasses need protocol semantics | Use as a rule-derived context tag only |
| HWE-Bench 5 classes | Semantic failure mode | **Weak**: almost every single-token mutant becomes "functional logic" | Low discriminative power at this scale |
| CWE-1194/1199 | Security weakness | Mostly N/A; reset-value → CWE-1221, FSM → CWE-1245 | Only if designs carry security assets |
| CirFix Cat 1/2 | Expert difficulty judgement | **No** (subjective) | Replace with measured covariates |

*Table: practicality assessment synthesized in the taxonomy notes (inference), built on the cited sources.*

Two coverage gaps matter for how you describe the benchmark. First, **missing logic is a large real-world class**. MSR'08's high-frequency patterns include additions (an `assign` statement added, an if-branch added), and Ma et al. count Failure-to-Update and Incomplete Implementation as distinct subclasses ([MSR'08](https://masc.soe.ucsc.edu/docs/msr08.pdf); [Ma et al.](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10355397)). Replace-only operators cannot produce these, but delete operators can. Second, **conceptual errors (19–39% in Al-Asaad's DLX/PUMA data) are by definition not single-token mutants** [full text, workshop version] ([Al-Asaad et al.](https://www.ece.ucdavis.edu/~halasaad/Data/hldvt97.pdf)). The software analogue agrees: Just et al. found 73% of 357 real Java faults were coupled to common mutants, leaving 27% that no simple operator modelled [abstract + slides] ([Just et al., FSE 2014](https://people.cs.umass.edu/%7Erjust/publ/mutants_real_faults_fse_2014.pdf)). For planning the miter filter, Fellner et al. found that **33.21% of Verilog mutants were equivalent on average, with a range of 0–95.3% across 16 models** [full text] ([arXiv 1907.07368](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.07368)). Expect large per-design variation in how many candidates SymbiYosys discards.

## Depth-difficulty rests on one table and a by-construction argument

The strongest hardware evidence is RTL-Repair's **Output/State Divergence Delta (OSDD)**: the number of cycles from the first internal state divergence to the first output divergence, plus one [full text] ([RTL-Repair, ASPLOS 2024](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10561674)). In the paper's own words, "Both RTL-Repair and CirFix were only able to solve benchmarks with low OSDD." The largest OSDD repaired was 25 (sdram_k2, fixed by static preprocessing), and the largest repaired by the unrolling synthesizer was 13 (i2c_k1). A tally of their Table 2 in the notes (inference, not stated by the authors) gives this picture. The three bugs with OSDD above 25 (73,346, 74,109, and 2,963 cycles) were repaired by **0 of 2 tools**. Among the 24 bugs at OSDD ≤ 25, RTL-Repair was correct on 16 and CirFix on 7. So **high depth predicted failure, but low depth did not predict success**. Those three deep bugs also come from the largest designs with the longest testbenches (tate pairing at 74,149 cycles, Reed-Solomon at 166,166), so depth and scale cannot be separated. The same table shows why depth is slippery. i2c_k1 diverged 13 cycles before failure, but repair needed only a 4-cycle window because the buggy register "was updated only four cycles in the past". Stateless decoder bugs have OSDD 0 yet still needed 10–20-cycle windows, because several input vectors are required to expose them.

The post-silicon QED line from Mitra's group treats short error-detection latency as a precondition for localization. That link holds largely by construction: trace buffers and IFRA's 1,024-entry recorders cannot localize what falls outside their window. IFRA reported 75% exact localization, 21% correct with candidates, and 4% missed over 800 injections, but **did not break accuracy down by latency** [full text, tech report] ([IFRA TR](https://web.stanford.edu/group/rsg_csl/twiki/pub/Public/ValidationProjectsPublications/IFRA_TR_1.2.pdf)). Symbolic QED localized 100% of bugs with traces of 13–29 cycles, which leaves no variance to correlate [full text] ([SQED](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.06541)). S2QED adds a warning that applies directly to a miter design: a bug needing a 14-instruction activation sequence was exposed by a 1-instruction trace from a symbolic initial state [full text] ([S2QED, DATE 2018](https://theory.stanford.edu/~barrett/pubs/FUN+18.pdf)). **Counterexample length depends on the initial-state assumption**, so a reset-initialized miter measures activation plus propagation from reset, which is a different quantity from OSDD.

On the LLM side, the closest result is Pecker (2026). It tiers 41 bugs as combinational, sequential, and industrial. When moving from the combinational to the sequential tier, Top-5 localization accuracy drops **from 92% to 56% for Tarsel and from 75% to 44% for Wit-HW** [full text, arXiv HTML] ([Pecker](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02583v1)). Its tiers also differ in code size (industrial designs run 420–4,366 LOC), and it never bins by measured activation-to-observation latency, even though it defines that very gap. VeriPilot builds on the "earliest divergent cycle" and claims its biggest gains on designs "with deep control and data dependencies", but reports no depth-binned numbers [full text] ([VeriPilot](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23759v1)). **No LLM debugging or repair paper found reports success as a function of cycles-to-failure, CEX length, or OSDD.**

Software analogues support the mechanism but not the size of the effect. Jahangirova et al. found **zero failed error propagation at unit level across 258,372 executions of Defects4J faults, but 11.4% at system level (95% CI about 8.8–14.4%)**. Mutants showed more propagation failure than real faults [full text] ([arXiv 2011.10787](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.10787)). Coincidental correctness is common and degrades coverage-based fault localization [summary only] ([Masri & Assi, TOSEM 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262164188_Prevalence_of_Coincidental_Correctness_and_Mitigation_of_its_Impact_on_Fault_Localization)). For agent repair, the dominant SWE-bench difficulty predictors are patch size and fragmentation. Al-Haque & Johnson predict agent success on 45,769 tasks with AUC 0.863 from static features, led by lines deleted, number of hunks, and gap between hunks [full text, arXiv HTML] ([arXiv 2608.18280](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18280)). Single-site mutants hold those axes constant at one hunk, which leaves propagation depth and design size as the remaining candidates (inference). Two cautions also transfer. Pearson et al. found fault-localization rankings on 2,995 artificial faults did not predict rankings on 310 real faults ([Pearson et al., ICSE 2017](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rjust/publ/fault_localization_effectiveness_icse_2017.pdf)). And predictive mutation testing's AUC above 0.8 shrank once trivially unreached mutants were removed ([arXiv 2005.11532](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11532)).

The verdict: **depth-difficulty is a plausible, mechanistically motivated hypothesis with no direct LLM evidence and one confounded hardware table.** The confounds below are documented in the sources.

| Confound | Evidence | Control in this benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Design size / testbench length | Only the very-high-OSDD bugs sit in the largest designs ([RTL-Repair](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10561674)); success falls with code scale (ARSP [snippet-only], [arXiv 2508.16517](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.16517)); HLS repair LOC vs. pass@1 ρ = −0.389 ([arXiv 2610.03971](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03971v1)) | Log LOC and flop count; analyse within design or with design as a cluster |
| Combinational vs. sequential | Pecker tier drops are mixed with LOC ([Pecker](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02583v1)) | Record per mutant, not just per design |
| Initial-state assumption | Symbolic start shortens traces ([S2QED](https://theory.stanford.edu/~barrett/pubs/FUN+18.pdf)) | State that depth is measured from reset |
| Divergence vs. last relevant update | i2c_k1: OSDD 13, window 4 ([RTL-Repair](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10561674)) | Log both CEX depth and the activation-to-observation gap |
| Depth-0 bugs needing multiple vectors | Decoders: OSDD 0, windows 10–20 ([RTL-Repair](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10561674)) | Do not treat depth 0 as "easy" by definition |
| Stimulus strength | Same bugs: EDL up to billions of cycles vs. hundreds under QED ([Fast QED](https://past.date-conference.com/proceedings-archive/2015/pdf/1012.pdf)) | If the agent gets a testbench, log its first-failure cycle separately from formal depth |
| Operator class | Stubbornness clusters by operator, e.g. logical-connector replacement [abstract only] ([Yao et al., ICSE 2014](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1508140/)) | Include operator family as a covariate |

One more inference shapes how to set expectations. VerilogEval/RTLLM designs are small, so their minimum CEX depths will mostly be a few cycles to low tens. That is below the OSDD > 25 regime where RTL-Repair's failures concentrated. **A weak or null depth effect is a likely outcome**, and it should be reported as a finding about the short-depth regime, not as a refutation.

## No existing RTL repair benchmark grades formally or crosses difficulty with scaffolding

Across the debug and repair benchmarks surveyed, "difficulty" is almost always a post-hoc pass rate or a proxy. CVDP is the one benchmark with human-tagged easy/medium/hard labels, and it publishes no tagging rubric and reports results by category, not difficulty ([CVDP](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14074)).

| Benchmark | Bugs | Difficulty axis | Grading | Agent vs. single-shot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CVDP cid16 (NVIDIA 2025) | 36 non-agentic + 11 agentic debug problems | Human easy/med/hard, unreported in results | CocoTB simulation | Agentic problems run single-turn only |
| RTLFixer (DAC 2024) | LLM syntax errors on VerilogEval | VerilogEval easy/hard by pass-rate threshold 0.1 | Compile + simulation | One-shot vs. ReAct; small gain for GPT-4 |
| CirFix (ASPLOS 2022) | 32 expert-seeded | Expert Cat 1/2 | Testbench + manual "correct" | N/A (genetic programming) |
| RTL-Repair (ASPLOS 2024) | CirFix + 12 real FPGA bugs | OSDD (post hoc) | Testbench + gate-level sim + manual A–D | N/A (symbolic) |
| MEIC | 178 injected into RTLLM | Syntax vs. function; per module | Testbench Fix Rate | Iterative vs. single GPT-4 |
| UVLLM | 331 injected (RTLLM-based) | Module type | Hit Rate vs. expert Fix Rate | Iterative pipeline |
| VeriDebug | ~8,000 GPT-4-injected | Code length; 13 bug types | Exact-match line | No |
| VeriPilot (2026) | Strider 57 + CVDP cid16 | Dataset (student vs. CVDP) | Testbench re-simulation | Direct LLM vs. MAGE agent vs. VeriPilot |
| VeriBugBench (2026) | 2,608 operator mutants | Source/project size only | Output-trace divergence filter | No LLM evaluation |

*Sources: [CVDP](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14074), [RTLFixer](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16543), [CirFix](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10421496), [RTL-Repair](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10561674), [MEIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06840), [UVLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16238), [VeriDebug](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19099v1), [VeriPilot](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23759v1), [VeriBugBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18022), [HWE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14709).*

Testbench overfitting is the documented weakness that formal grading answers. On RTL-Repair's real bugs, **9 of 12 repairs passed the provided testbench but only 2 fully matched the ground truth** ([RTL-Repair](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10561674)). UVLLM reports that other methods show more than 30% deviation between output-match Hit Rate and expert-verified Fix Rate on functional bugs ([UVLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16238)). CirFix produced 21 plausible patches but only 16 correct ones ([CirFix record](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/DBLP_conf_asplos_Ahmad0W22.html)). Formal equivalence does appear nearby, but never as a repair-benchmark grader. NotSoTiny and RealBench use Yosys or JasperGold equivalence to grade *generation*, and FormalRTL and a 2026 SymbiYosys/Z3 pipeline feed counterexamples back to repair agents as *feedback* [snippet-only for these four] ([NotSoTiny](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20823); [RealBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.16200); [FormalRTL](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08738v1); [arXiv 2607.28877](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28877v1)). Name these in an interview before an interviewer does. They are the adjacent work that the novelty claim must stand next to.

Evidence on whether scaffolding helps more on hard bugs is thin but points one way. VeriPilot's agent gain over direct GPT-5 was **+8.8 points on student-assignment bugs and +17.2 on the harder CVDP debug subset** ([VeriPilot](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23759v1)). That is two datasets in one paper, and its GPT-4o baseline is internally inconsistent (54.3% in the abstract, 57.14% in Table I). RTLFixer's ReAct gain was large for GPT-3.5 but about one point for GPT-4 ([RTLFixer](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16543)). On SWE-bench Verified, the relative advantage of top agentic systems over Agentless grew from about 1.14x on easy tasks to 1.5–1.8x on hard ones, but models and scaffolds differ between entries, so it is suggestive only [secondary: blog analysis] ([Ganhotra](https://jatinganhotra.dev/blog/swe-agents/2025/04/15/swe-bench-verified-easy-medium-hard.html)). **No controlled same-model study of agent vs. single-shot by difficulty exists in RTL or software.** That leaves three novelty claims, each supported only by the absence of results after targeted searches through October 2026: formal-equivalence grading of LLM RTL repair, minimum miter-CEX depth as a difficulty axis, and the agent-vs-single-shot gap reported by depth stratum. One design caveat (inference): EQY demands equivalence with the golden design, so a fix that is correct under the spec but differs in unspecified behaviour would be marked wrong. The spec rewrite should therefore pin down reset values and corner cases.

## Disguise cuts lexical recall, but formal grading still pays out memorized designs

Contamination of the source designs is measured, not hypothetical. VeriContaminated (ICLAD 2025) reports CDD contamination signals of **100% on RTLLM and 98.72% on VerilogEval for GPT-3.5, and 100%/100% for GPT-4o**. Open models scored lower (DeepSeek-Coder 58%/26.92%) [full text, arXiv HTML] ([VeriContaminated](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13572v2)). Its two detectors disagree heavily for mid-sized models: LLaMA 2 scores 17.95% on CDD but 67.95% on Min-K% for VerilogEval. So treat the numbers as signals, not ground truth. VerilogEval's 156 problems come from HDLBits, and many public GitHub repositories post HDLBits solutions ([VerilogEval](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.07544); [GitHub topic](https://github.com/topics/hdl-bits-solutions)). NotSoTiny found RTLLM had consistently higher Min-K% values than its own fresh Tiny Tapeout designs ([NotSoTiny](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20823v1)). LiveVerilogEval (DATE 2026) reports "significant performance degradation" on semantically mutated designs [snippet-only; the PDF was unreachable] ([DATE 2026](https://past.date-conference.com/proceedings-archive/2026/DATA/167.pdf)).

Debugging is more exposed than generation, and EQY grading sharpens the problem (inference). A model that recognizes a counter or FIFO can "repair" it by writing out the memorized golden version, and a formal-equivalence grader awards full credit because the result *is* equivalent. Contamination here works as an answer key. The software evidence says the level-2 disguise (renaming, spec rewrite, light restructuring) will blunt but not remove this:

| Perturbation | Measured effect | Source and flag |
|---|---|---|
| Semantics-preserving transforms on Defects4J/GitBug-Java **repair** | −4.1 pts (GPT-4o) to −15.98 (Llama-3.1); drop correlates with per-item NLL | [arXiv 2604.21579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21579) [abstract]; "renaming matters mainly combined with structural change" [snippet-only] |
| Alpha-renaming, execution prediction | −0.7 to −14.9 pts on LiveCodeBench; ambiguous names up to −23.8 | [arXiv 2510.03178](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03178v1) [full text] |
| Training on *paraphrased* test data | 7B/13B Llama approached GPT-4 on HumanEval; n-gram decontamination missed it | [arXiv 2311.04850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.04850) [full text] |
| LLM-evolved problems (EvoEval) | −39.4% pass@1 average across 51 LLMs, but difficulty also changed | [arXiv 2403.19114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19114v1) [abstract] |
| Filtering leakage and weak tests (SWE-bench+) | Resolution 12.47% → 3.97% | [arXiv 2410.06992](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06992v2) [full text] |

The pattern is that **surface changes cost frontier models a few points, while changes to semantics or provenance cost far more**. In practice that means altering widths, parameters, FIFO depths, counter moduli, FSM encodings, or adding a small feature, and including a few freshly written designs. Whether contamination biases the *agent-minus-baseline gap* when both arms use one model is unresolved; no study measures it. Two plausible mechanisms push in opposite directions (inference). Recall could saturate the single-shot baseline and compress the gap. Or multi-turn simulation output could give the agent more cues to recognize the design and revert it, inflating the gap. Agents also have leakage channels of their own: web retrieval during evaluation inflates scores even on clean benchmarks ([arXiv 2608.05179](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05179)), and agents reportedly recover fixes via `git log` [snippet-only, attribution uncertain] ([SWE Atlas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08366)). MedicalAgentsBench offers a precedent for screening memorization before comparing scaffolds ([arXiv 2503.07459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07459)).

The cheap detection methods are black-box. Golchin & Surdeanu's guided-instruction completion probe reached **92–100% agreement with human experts** across seven datasets [full text] ([Time Travel, ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.08493v1)). SWE-Bench Illusion's identity probe found models named the buggy file from the issue alone with up to 76% accuracy on SWE-bench Verified, against 53% on outside repositories ([arXiv 2506.12286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286)). Neither has been validated on Verilog, and no HDL thresholds exist.

## A practical plan: one label stack, one pre-registered hypothesis, eight logged covariates

**Taxonomy.** Label every mutant deterministically at mutation time with three fields. The first is the VeriBugBench/Meng family (Expression, Bit-vector & access, Assignment, Timing, Port). The second is the MSR'08 pattern short name, such as IF_CC for a relational swap inside an `if` condition or NB_CE for a right-hand-side change under `<=`. The third is a rule-derived context tag: reset branch, FSM next-state, condition, data-path right-hand side, or timing (edge or blocking/non-blocking). This costs nothing at runtime and is defensible because each layer cites a real-bug study. Report your operator mix next to Meng's prevalence (53.8/21.4/15.1/3.3/1.3%) so reviewers can see how "realistic" it is. Include delete operators so missing-assignment and missing-branch bugs are represented. If you ever add Ma- or HWE-Bench-style semantic labels from an LLM, hand-label a subset and report Cohen's κ. That recommendation is inference, motivated by RemembERR's roughly 80% human agreement.

**Depth framing.** State it as a pre-registered hypothesis: *repair and localization success decrease with minimum miter-CEX depth from reset, and the agent's advantage over single-shot grows with depth, after controlling for design size, operator family, and sequentiality.* Cite RTL-Repair's OSDD as motivation, not proof. With about 15 designs, keep the analysis simple. Show success rate by arm in depth bins (for example 0, 1–2, 3–5, 6+), report per-design counts so no single design dominates a bin, and fit one logistic regression (success ~ depth × arm + log flops + family + comb/seq, with design as a cluster or fixed effect). The arm × depth interaction is the scientifically interesting term. Report localization (did the fix touch the mutated statement?) and repair (EQY pass) as separate endpoints. The software literature suggests mutants may be easy to repair once found but harder to localize, and those effects can cancel in a single score ([BugFarm](https://alirezai.cs.illinois.edu/assets/pdf/bugfarm.pdf); [Jahangirova et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.10787)).

**Covariates.** Every item below falls out of tools already in the pipeline.

| Covariate | How to log it | Why (evidence status) |
|---|---|---|
| Operator family + MSR'08 pattern + context tag | Emitted by the mutator | Hardness clusters by operator (Yao et al., [abstract only](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1508140/)); VeriDebug per-type generation accuracy ranges 0.39 ("initial") to 1.00 ("signal") ([full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19099v1)) |
| Minimum CEX depth | SBY BMC step at which the miter assertion first fails | Novel difficulty axis; hypothesis |
| Activation-to-observation gap | In the CEX trace: first cycle any register differs between the two miter copies vs. first output mismatch | Direct OSDD analogue ([RTL-Repair, full text](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10561674)) |
| Design size and sequentiality | LOC, `yosys stat` flop/cell counts, comb/seq flag per design and per mutation site | Primary confound (RTL-Repair, Pecker, HLS ρ = −0.389) |
| Observability | Number of output bits diverging in the CEX; first-failure cycle in any testbench given to the agent | Propagation and stimulus effects ([FEP study](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.10787); [Fast QED](https://past.date-conference.com/proceedings-archive/2015/pdf/1012.pdf)) |
| Contamination probe | Per design: identity probe (name the original problem) and completion probe on original vs. disguised RTL | VeriContaminated; SWE-Bench Illusion |
| Revert-to-golden flag | Edit distance of the model's fix to the minimal reverse mutation vs. to the full public original | Inference: catches recall that EQY rewards |
| Agent effort | Tool calls, simulations run, tokens, wall-clock time | Engineering choice: enables cost-normalized agent vs. single-shot comparison |

**Contamination controls.** Run the agent with no network and with no original RTL or git history in the sandbox. Make at least one semantic change per design (width, parameter, encoding) on top of the level-2 disguise. Add two or three hand-written designs as a fresh control stratum, and report the agent-minus-baseline gap separately on disguised-public and fresh items. A gap that holds on fresh items is credible; one that appears only on public-source items is suspect.

In an interview, the defensible positions follow from the evidence. The taxonomy choice is grounded in real-bug mining studies. Depth is an explicit hypothesis with named confounds, not a claimed law. Formal grading answers a documented overfitting problem. And contamination is measured per design rather than assumed away. Claims to avoid are that depth "is known to" predict LLM difficulty, that renaming "removes" contamination, or that mutant results transfer to real bugs; Pearson et al.'s non-transfer result is the standard rebuttal to that last one.

## Conclusion

The research changes what the benchmark is *for*. Absolute difficulty prediction is a crowded, noisy target, and single-site mutants strip out the patch-size and fragmentation features that dominate software difficulty models. What remains distinctive is the interaction: whether tool use (simulation, waveforms, iterative CEX inspection) buys more on bugs whose cause and symptom are separated in time. No published RTL or software study has measured that with the model held fixed. A clean null in the short-depth regime of small designs would still be the first depth-binned LLM debugging curve in the literature.

The second shift is that formal grading, usually presented as pure rigor, changes the contamination calculus. Testbench grading lets overfit patches through. Equivalence grading closes that hole but opens another, because a model that recalls the golden design earns full credit. The two problems trade places rather than disappearing. That is why a per-design revert-to-golden check and a small fresh-design stratum are not optional extras but the controls that make an EQY-graded result interpretable.
